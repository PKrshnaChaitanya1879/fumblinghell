Research

Research Intern, School of Engineering Sciences & Technology, University of Hyderabad

Under Prof. Dibakar Das · 18 May – 18 June 2026

A one-month research internship synthesising lead-free KNN (K₀.₄₈Na₀.₅₂NbO₃) piezoceramics by the solid-state route.

What I did

  • Did the weighing, wet ball milling, calcination, pressing, binder burnout and sintering myself. I prepared the XRD samples; the lab ran the scans.
  • Wet ball milling in ethanol (5:1 ball-to-powder ratio), then two calcination batches: B1 at 825 °C for 4 h and B2 at 900 °C for 2 h.
  • Pressed pellets in an 8 mm die at 2 t, burnt out the PVA binder at 600 °C for 4 h, and sintered them in pack powder inside covered crucibles.

Results

  • XRD of both calcined powders shows the main orthorhombic KNN perovskite phase plus a minor K₂Nb₄O₁₁ secondary phase.
  • Sintering at 1000 °C for 2 h did not densify the pellets (63–64%). At 1100 °C for 4 h the best pellets reached 88.7% (B1) and 91.4% (B2).
  • All densities were measured geometrically, from caliper dimensions and mass.

Not yet done: XRD of the sintered pellets, Archimedes density, and electrical or piezoelectric tests, which need above 95% density.

Propellant bench tests

With four teammates · August 2026 · small PVC casings, 2.5 to 3 cm long

  • Compared two ways of preparing the potassium nitrate/sucrose fuel, with iron(III) oxide as a catalyst: molten fuel poured straight into the casing, and fuel ground for 20 minutes then compressed into it.
  • The poured test failed: the fuel was contaminated by the detergent used as sealant.
  • The ground and compressed grain burned for about 9 s. Slower than ideal because of moisture, though the catalyst produced good sparks.
  • Fix identified: dry for 12 hours at 60 °C and pack more densely.
